What was measured

Peak is the loudest sample; anything over 0 dBFS is clipped by the speakers. RMS is average level, which is roughly loudness. "Seam" is the jump at the loop point of the engine's noise buffer, as a multiple of an ordinary sample-to-sample step: anything well above 1× is a click.

What changed since Before

  • Mix. A glue compressor and a limiter on the output, so stacked hits no longer clip. The music ducks under crashes, nitro and the finish. The engine's sub is halved and high-passed at 32 Hz.
  • Music. Notes are booked on the audio clock ahead of time instead of fired from a timer, so the rhythm no longer depends on the frame rate. The 4-bar loop is now an 8-bar form: the bell line rotates, the second half lifts an octave, the eighth bar ends on a taiko fill. The drone is one continuous voice instead of a re-struck note every bar.
  • Engine and road. The engine's character is unchanged, with an exhaust crack on upshifts under load. New: wind that rises with the square of speed, tyre rumble on tarmac, gravel off-road, and a tyre squeal driven by the car's side-slip.
  • Effects. Square-wave beeps replaced with chimes and hits from the music's own bell partials. Gems climb the Hirajoshi scale as you chain them. Every repeated hit varies its pitch by ±2.5%. The finish fanfare runs on the audio clock, not setTimeout.
  • Recordings. Seven effects can use CC0 recordings from Kenney's Impact and Sci-fi packs: crash, car contact, landing, rock smash, monster hit, nitro and the mech transform. Each has several takes and never repeats the last one. Every take is trimmed, mono AAC, 289 kB for all 32. If a browser cannot decode one, the synthesised voice plays instead.
  • Levels. Matched by measurement, not by ear: the lap and the music sit within 1 dB of the old mix, and each recording within 3 dB RMS of the synthesised voice it replaces.
  • Loops and tabs. Noise buffers loop without a click. A hidden tab now suspends the audio along with the race, instead of droning on.
